# PRD Quality Review — GymOS PRD (V1.5 "Beta-Ready" update)

## Overall verdict

The V1.5 layer is substantively strong where it counts most: concrete numeric acceptance criteria throughout (rate limits, timeouts, cutoffs), disciplined counter-metrics on every V1.5 success metric, and unusually honest admissions for a PRD (Notch Pay "never actually carried live traffic," a payment spike run against a "stand-in business account" rather than GymOS's own). Two structural gaps hold it back from ship-ready, both concentrated in the new Flow B billing area (FR-130–FR-138): that ~15-FR feature has no Goal, Success Metric, or Release Definition gate, so no one can say whether "gym pays GymOS" is meant to be proven working by beta's end; and FR-133 states automated recurring SaaS billing as settled mechanics when OQ-14 says the underlying mechanism (auto-debit vs. approval-request job) is still genuinely unresolved. Downstream usability is thin — no glossary anywhere in the document, and UJ-10 breaks the named-protagonist convention the other nine journeys follow. None of this blocks the V1.0 portion, which was lightly reviewed here per the brief and shows no new issues.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

The Open Questions table is a genuine strength: "Resolved" rows carry real rationale (OQ-8's storage-growth precedent, OQ-11's one-owner decision), and OQ-7/OQ-12/OQ-13/OQ-14 are left honestly open rather than dressed up as answered. The Addendum's Key Architectural Decisions table gives trade-off rationale, not just resolutions. But two places let a stated requirement outrun its own open question, which is exactly the "smoothed to neutral" failure mode the rubric warns about:

### Findings
- **critical** Flow B (gym→GymOS SaaS billing) has no Goal, Success Metric, or Release Definition gate (§3.1, §3.2, §10) — FR-130–FR-138 build a full billing lifecycle (`active → past_due → grace_period → suspended`), a Super Admin Billing view, and tenant-suspension enforcement (NFR-018), but none of G-7–G-11, none of the 11 rows in §3.2, and none of the 5 items in §10's beta-ready checklist mention it. A decision-maker cannot tell whether Flow B is in scope for the beta acceptance bar or is being built now but validated later. This gap is compounded by FR-136 (beta accommodation): if Super Admin comps most beta gyms' SaaS fees "so beta gyms aren't charged during validation," the billing machinery may never be exercised with real money and real failure/retry paths during the beta window at all — which would make its absence from the release definition the correct call, but the PRD never says so explicitly either way. *Fix:* add a G-# for platform billing (or explicitly fold it under an existing goal), add a §3.2 success metric (e.g., "SaaS billing collection success rate" with a counter-metric for false suspensions), and add a 6th item to §10's checklist stating whether Flow B correctness is a beta gate or is deliberately excluded pending a later cohort.
- **high** FR-133 states automated SaaS billing retries as settled mechanics ("the system retries on a defined schedule") while OQ-14 — two pages later — says the fundamental shape is still unknown: "automated recurring debits, or a fresh collect each SaaS cycle the Owner approves? ... the answer materially changes Flow B's design (a collection job vs. a reminder-to-approve job)." A reader implementing FR-133 in isolation has no signal that its premise is contingent; the caveat lives only in OQ-7's row, not in FR-133 or FR-130. *Fix:* add "(contingent on OQ-14 — see §9)" to FR-130 and FR-133, or mark them "Amendment pending OQ-14 resolution" the way the doc already flags other contingent items.
- **high** FR-100 discloses that the Tara Money spike "passed in full against a stand-in business account," with GymOS's own account (`9FmIZg9GBB`) "previously blocked on account activation," and that re-verification against the real account is "the remaining step before full production reliance" (OQ-7). Given FR-099 simultaneously claims Tara Money "has been the active, live-proven... provider since Story 4.2," and FR-124 states Flow A payments settle "directly into that gym's own Tara Money account," the PRD leaves unresolved exactly whose account has been receiving real member payments during the interim period, and whether that reconciles cleanly with FR-124's no-aggregation principle. FR-102's cutover procedure (with its NFR-012 zero-discrepancy bar) covers the Notch Pay→Tara Money provider swap only — it does not cover a stand-in-account→real-account money swap, which carries the same double-charge/lost-payment risk profile. *Fix:* state explicitly which account has held live funds since Story 4.2, and whether the FR-102/NFR-012 reconciliation rigor also applies to the pending account swap once OQ-7 resolves.

## Substance over theater — strong

Numeric thresholds are everywhere and specific to the product (rate limits: 2/day, 3-hr gap for N-06 in FR-114; taps: max 3 for a straight-through renewal in FR-050; latency: <3s in FR-052; auto-dismiss: 30 min default in FR-051). Story/decision-log citations (Story 2.6, Story 4.2, Story 1.13, `9FmIZg9GBB`) are concrete rather than invented specificity, and the PRD is willing to admit failure honestly — "Notch Pay... never actually carried live traffic" and NFR-005's refusal to over-promise availability ("No additional uptime commitment is made until commercial scale") are the opposite of NFR theater. No boilerplate "must be scalable/secure/reliable" language was found anywhere in the document.

### Findings
- **low** G-11 ("Prove classes and workout plans work... without adding operational load") has no corresponding success metric measuring operational load — §3.2 has a class-booking reliability metric but nothing that would falsify or confirm the "no added load" half of the goal. *Fix:* either add a light metric (e.g., staff-reported minutes/week on class admin) or soften the goal to what's actually measured.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The retention-loop thesis (§1, three-loop table) is real and load-bearing — V1.5's two theses (operational self-sufficiency and between-visit engagement) are named explicitly in §1.1 and traceable through to FRs. Counter-metrics are present for every V1.5 success metric in §3.2, which is real discipline, not box-ticking. The gap is the same one flagged under decision-readiness: Flow B billing is a substantial, novel-in-V1.5 capability that sits outside the stated thesis and outside the metrics/goals framework entirely, which reads as a scope item that arrived without being integrated into the PRD's own accounting system.

## Done-ness clarity — strong

V1.5 FRs are unusually testable: FR-087's role-ceiling rule, FR-090's server-side revocation check, FR-095's five-bullet RLS visibility rule, FR-114's rate-limit numbers, NFR-013's explicit CI assertions (an Owner cannot mint a Super Admin, etc.) all give an engineer a clear verification target. A search for vague done-ness language ("gracefully," "reasonable," "user-friendly") found exactly one hit in FR-128 ("initiation fails gracefully"), and it is immediately followed by a concrete consequence ("directing the member to the desk, and the Owner is notified"), so it does not read as empty.

### Findings
- **high** (cross-referenced from Decision-readiness above) FR-133's premise of settled automated billing mechanics conflicts with OQ-14's open question about the billing mechanism's fundamental shape — an engineer building to FR-133 today does not know if they're building a collection job or a reminder-to-approve job.

## Scope honesty — strong

§8's Out of Scope table is thorough and specific (waitlists, coach-to-receptionist escalation, provider-executed refunds all named with target versions), and the "Explicit non-goal" callout on Manager/Owner progress-data visibility is exactly the kind of honest, permanent de-scoping the rubric rewards — it's framed as a product commitment requiring a recorded decision to reverse, not a soft deferral. FR-136's beta accommodation is disclosed openly rather than silently assumed. The PRD does not use the literal `[ASSUMPTION]` / `[NOTE FOR PM]` tag syntax anywhere in the document (V1.0 or V1.5), but it substitutes functionally via "Resolved" OQ rows carrying rationale and the Addendum's Key Architectural Decisions table — see Mechanical notes.

## Downstream usability — thin

No Glossary section exists anywhere in the ~900-line document. Domain terms (`grace_period`, `past_due`, `pending_activation`, "billing anchor") are mostly defined inline at first use via state-machine tables (FR-027, FR-131), which is adequate but not centrally indexed — a downstream architecture or story-writing pass has to reconstruct definitions by searching rather than looking them up. FR/NFR IDs are globally unique with no duplicates or unresolved cross-references found.

### Findings
- **medium** No Glossary section. Given this PRD is chain-top (feeds architecture and story creation per its "Development Team" audience and BMad planning-artifacts placement), a term index would materially help downstream extraction. *Fix:* add a short Glossary compiling the state-machine values and any gym/platform-specific vocabulary already scattered across FR-027, FR-131, FR-064's role table.
- **medium** UJ-10 ("The payment provider cutover," §5) has no named protagonist and isn't a user journey in the sense the other nine are — it's a system/migration narrative ("the swap is one config surface behind the `PaymentProvider` interface"). It breaks the pattern the rubric flags ("no floating UJs") and would read more naturally as an Addendum decision-log entry alongside the Tara Money rows already there in Addendum §D. *Fix:* move UJ-10's content to the Addendum or recast it around a person (e.g., the receptionist processing a payment during the cutover window) if it needs to stay a UJ.
- **low** FR numbering is not monotonic with document order: §6.20 (FR-124–FR-138) sits between §6.19 (FR-099–FR-103) and §6.21 (FR-104–FR-108) in the document, so FR numbers count up, jump to 124–138, then drop back to 104 before resuming upward. This is consistent with the PRD's stated append-to-section-in-authoring-order convention, but a reader skimming top-to-bottom for "what's the highest FR so far" will be misled at this one seam. *Fix:* a one-line note where §6.20 begins, similar to the existing intro callout about FR-071/FR-082, would resolve the confusion cheaply.

## Shape fit — strong

This is a multi-stakeholder B2B2C product (gym operators + consumer-facing member app), and UJs with named protagonists are correctly load-bearing here — nine of ten journeys have one, and V1.0 vs. V1.5 journeys are cleanly distinguished with inline "(V1.5)" tags (UJ-6 through UJ-10). Brownfield references are specific and checkable (Story 2.6, Story 4.2, Story 1.13), which is the right brownfield discipline per the rubric.

### Findings
- **low** Supervisor is a genuinely new V1.5 role (§4.1, §4.3) but has no persona paragraph in §4.2 alongside Owner/Manager, Receptionist, Member, Coach, and Super Admin, and no dedicated UJ (UJ-6 is about the Owner staffing, not a Supervisor's own workflow). Low stakes since Supervisor's capabilities mirror Owner's, but worth a one-paragraph addition for completeness before this feeds UX work.

## Mechanical notes

- **Stale persona text**: §4.2's Coach profile still reads "Workout plans and class scheduling are deferred to V1.5" — but this document is V1.5, and §6.21/§6.22 (FR-104–FR-112) now deliver both. This sentence was accurate when written for V1.0 and should have been updated for the V1.5 pass; unlike FR-054/FR-056 (correctly preserved as immutable V1.0-dated requirement text), §4.2 is a living current-state description that readers will take at face value. *Fix:* update §4.2's Coach paragraph to reflect V1.5 delivery.
- **No `[ASSUMPTION]` / `[NOTE FOR PM]` tags** anywhere in the document. Functionally substituted by "Resolved" OQ rows (§9) and the Addendum's Key Architectural Decisions table, which carry equivalent rationale — adequate substitute, but any downstream tooling expecting the standard tag convention (e.g., an Assumptions Index roundtrip check) will find nothing to roundtrip.
- **ID continuity**: FR-001–FR-138 and NFR-001–NFR-018 are each globally unique with no gaps or duplicates found; cross-references (e.g., FR-095→FR-055, NFR-018→FR-131/FR-132, OQ-7→OQ-13/OQ-14) all resolve to real, matching entries. The one ordering wrinkle is the §6.20 FR-number seam noted above.
- **Glossary drift**: none detected — domain nouns (grace_period, expiring_soon, past_due, pending_activation) are used identically everywhere they appear, including across the Addendum.
