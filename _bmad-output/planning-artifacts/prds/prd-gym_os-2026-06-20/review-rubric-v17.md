# PRD Quality Review — GymOS PRD, V1.7 additions

Scope: Section 1.3, G-16..G-18 and the four V1.7 metric rows, 4.5, UJ-12, 6.30-6.32 (FR-175..FR-203), NFR-023..NFR-025, Section 8 rows touched, OQ-26 (method) and OQ-27..OQ-30, 10.2, glossary additions, Section 12, addendum H. Consistency checked against FR-072, FR-087..FR-092, FR-126/127, FR-132, NFR-001, NFR-013, NFR-019, NFR-021. Prior pass: review-rubric-v16.md.

## Overall verdict

V1.7 has a clear spine (support staff first, because it unlocks onboarding, then pages, then directory), the delivery order is a stated decision with its rationale, and the support-staff slice is specific where it matters (many-to-many assignment, one flag, one Owner row, AAL2 in the database, next-request revocation). What is at risk: the support slice grants the platform its most powerful capability (replace a gym's payment account, refund, waive) while the Owner is deliberately blinded to who acts, and that trade-off is not argued; landing pages and directory have no journeys and several unbounded verbs ("promptly", "fast", "first-class"); and the items Section 12 G says "gate" the support release are not in the 10.2 gate and have no IDs.

Dimension judgments: Decision-readiness adequate; Substance over theater adequate; Strategic coherence adequate; Done-ness clarity thin; Scope honesty adequate; Downstream usability adequate; Shape fit adequate.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Good: delivery order, TOTP-over-OTP rationale (Section 12 E: "a code sent over either channel is not an independent factor"), the rejected hidden-account alternative, and OQ-30 flagged with an inline ASSUMPTION in FR-180. Open questions are real (OQ-28, OQ-29, OQ-30 each block named stories).

Two trade-offs are smoothed over. First, FR-178 + FR-182 + FR-183 give an anonymous "GymOS Support" actor the ability to connect or replace the gym's Tara Money account and to record refunds and waivers, with the Owner seeing only a collapsed label and (per FR-183) support "excluded from ... notifications". Nothing records that the cost of this design is that the Owner cannot attribute, or be told about, the highest-risk change in the product. Second, FR-180 makes access "on by default for every gym", which at release includes gyms that are live with real customers (see project state: production onboarding since 2026-09); the PRD treats default-on as a given, not as a consent change for existing tenants.

### Findings
- **high** Payment-account replacement and money-moving actions by an unattributed actor, with no Owner-visible signal (FR-178, FR-182, FR-183, NFR-019) — FR-178 lets support "connect or replace the gym's Tara Money account" and "record payments, refunds, and registration-fee waivers"; the audit row "records that credentials changed, never their value"; the Owner sees "GymOS Support" and nothing individual. Replacing credentials is the one change that redirects a gym's money (the NFR-019 risk). The PRD does not say whether the Owner is notified, whether this needs a second approver, or whether it is accepted as a risk. *Fix:* add an FR: an Owner-facing notification/audit line on payment-account change and on refunds above a stated threshold, or state explicitly that it is accepted and why; add the attack case to the Support-access safety metric.
- **high** Default-on for existing live gyms is a retroactive consent change with no notice (FR-180, §10.2 step 1) — "on by default for every gym" has no clause for existing gyms: no announcement, no first-visit banner, no choice window. OQ-26 recorded "on by default and a gym may opt out" but never considered the existing-tenant case that V1.6 handled explicitly (FR-157: off for existing, on for new). *Fix:* decide existing-gym default (on with a one-time Owner notice, or off until Super Admin asks) and write it into FR-180 and the 10.2 gate.
- **medium** FR-180 states "switching it on restores the gym's assignments" as fact while OQ-30 is open — the ASSUMPTION tag is present, but FR-186, 10.2 step 1 item 3 and addendum H all rely on it. Restore-on-reenable also silently re-grants every historic assignee. *Fix:* resolve OQ-30 before the PRD is called final, or carry both outcomes in the gate.

## Substance over theater — adequate

Not theater: the FRs carry real rules (reserved-name list, 100-character about minimum, rename limits). Two weaker spots: FR-200 reads partly as a checklist ("clean semantic markup", "machine-readable site summary for AI crawlers"), and Section 12 G is a deferred-work triage (call-site counts, "line cites approximate", "the full report is a working file") sitting in the requirements document where the addendum would hold it.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-200 lists optimization techniques, not outcomes (FR-200) — "first-class requirement" is an adjective; "clean semantic markup" and "a machine-readable site summary" have no pass condition. *Fix:* name verifiable outcomes: structured data validates with zero errors in the stated validator, sitemap lists exactly the indexable pages, hreflang pairs reciprocal, summary file reachable at a stated path.
- **low** Section 12 G is backlog triage, not requirements (§12 G) — it cites a working file the reader may not have and says "about 30 call sites". Keep only what gates V1.7 (and give those IDs, see Done-ness); move the rest to addendum.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis (help from the inside, then public presence, then discovery) is coherent and ordering is argued. Weak links: G-16 has only safety metrics (Support-access safety, Revocation latency) and no outcome metric that says support actually reduces setup effort; the existing "Beta gym self-sufficiency" row is not tied to it. G-18 bets on directory discovery while NFR-009 puts pilot scale at 1-3 gyms, and OQ-29 (the thin-page threshold) is unresolved: at launch every Cameroon listing may be below any sensible threshold and therefore noindexed (FR-201), so the directory could ship with no search value. The PRD does not say what the page is for the gym: FR-189/190 give no call to action (app download, invite, contact), and "booking or payment on the public page" is deferred, so a published page is a brochure with no measured lead outcome.

### Findings
- **medium** G-16 lacks an outcome metric and a target for its own counter-metric (§3.1 G-16, §3.2 rows "Support-access safety", "Revocation latency") — the counter-metric "Time for a support person to complete a gym set-up request" has no baseline or target, and nothing measures reduced founder tickets. *Fix:* add one benefit metric (e.g. median days from gym creation to first member imported, or founder-support tickets for gyms with support vs without).
- **medium** Revocation-latency "metric" is a restatement of FR-186 (§3.2) — "refused on their very next request" is binary and CI-verifiable, not a production metric. *Fix:* fold into the Support-access safety row as a CI assertion, or measure observed revocation-to-refusal time.
- **medium** Directory thesis vs pilot scale and OQ-29 (G-18, FR-201, NFR-009) — with 1-3 gyms the thin-page rule may exclude everything. *Fix:* decide the threshold now, state the launch expectation ("at launch only individual gym pages are indexed"), and make the directory metric conditional on gyms count.
- **medium** Directory metrics are not controllable or complete (§3.2 "Directory discoverability") — "≥ 80% of published pages indexed within 30 days" depends on a search engine's behavior; there is no AI-search measure although G-18 names AI-search; the counter-metric "Thin pages indexed" is a defect count, not a trade-off. *Fix:* measure what GymOS controls (valid, submitted, crawlable) and add an AI-crawler access check; make the counter-metric something that can pull against indexing volume (e.g. share of pages indexed that have a content gate failure).
- **low** Landing pages have no stated purpose beyond existence (FR-189, FR-190) — no CTA or lead path, and Out of Scope defers analytics. *Fix:* one sentence on what a visitor can do (call, WhatsApp, get directions) and one metric if any.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

This is the weak dimension. The support FRs are mostly checkable (FR-184, FR-185, FR-186, FR-188). The public-site FRs lean on undefined timing and adjectives, and the invariant behind the Landing-page adoption metric is contradicted by how FR-191 is written.

### Findings
- **high** The publish gate holds only at the moment of publishing (FR-191, §3.2 "Landing-page adoption", FR-192, FR-198) — the metric says "every published page meets the publish gate", but FR-191 only says a page "can be published only when" the fields exist. Nothing says what happens when a published page later loses a required field: the only public plan is made private (FR-192), a plan is deleted, the logo is removed (Section 12 G notes "remove logo" gaps), or the country/city is blanked. *Fix:* state the invariant (gate re-checked on every edit; an edit that would break it is refused, or the page auto-unpublishes with Owner notice) and test it.
- **high** Takedown and visibility timing is unbounded (FR-198, FR-203, FR-195) — FR-203: "leaves the directory, the sitemap, and its listings promptly"; FR-198 gives no latency at all for suspension/unpublish. Caching in the public site makes this the exact place stories will guess. *Fix:* a number (e.g. "within 60 seconds on the page, within 24 hours in the sitemap") and what the address returns (404 vs 410 vs neutral page; FR-132 requires that the member-facing surface never mention billing, so a suspended gym's public address must not either).
- **high** Release gate omits items Section 12 G says "gate it" (§12 G first list, §10.2 Step 1 item 7, NFR-023) — the null-safe role check, the payment INSERT `member_id`/`gym_id` check, the `apps/landing` i18n parity gate, support account recovery (password-reset OTP) are listed as "Fix inside the support release (they gate it)" but have no FR/NFR and are not in 10.2. NFR-023's "closes the remaining policies and RPCs that read the role claim only" has no inventory, so "complete" (10.2 item 7) cannot be verified. *Fix:* give each gating item an ID or fold it into NFR-023 as a bullet list, and define completion testably (e.g. CI fails if any policy or RPC reference the role claim for an authorization decision, with an allowlist).
- **high** FR-178 does not define the delta over Supervisor (FR-177, FR-178, §4.1, §4.5) — support is "Supervisor scope" but "can do anything a Supervisor can, plus" config, Tara Money, transactions. §4.1 already says Supervisor has "the same staff-management and Settings access as Owner", and FR-087 limits staff creation. An architect cannot tell whether support is a distinct role value or a Supervisor with an extra flag-gated permission set, or which Owner-only actions Supervisor lacks today. *Fix:* a table of actions with Owner / Supervisor / Support columns, including "delete gym", "SaaS billing", "Allow GymOS Support", "rename page name".
- **medium** FR-195: "redirects permanently ... for 12 months" is self-contradictory (FR-195) — permanent redirect vs 12-month window; also "twice a year" has no calendar or rolling definition. *Fix:* pick one (e.g. "301 for 12 months, then name is releasable"), define the year.
- **medium** FR-195 vs FR-190 on who claims a name — claim is "by the gym's Owner at publish time", yet FR-190 and §4.5 let a Supervisor or support person publish. Also unstated: whether a name is released when the page is unpublished or the gym is suspended (FR-198 says only that the address stops resolving). *Fix:* state who may claim, and the name's lifecycle on unpublish/suspend.
- **medium** FR-201 / FR-199 geography undefined (FR-199, FR-191) — "country and city" are required fields but it is not stated whether they are free text or controlled values; free text yields "Douala" / "Duala" / "douala" as separate listings and defeats the threshold rule. *Fix:* controlled list or normalization rule.
- **medium** NFR-025 target is under-specified (NFR-025) — "largest content paint is within 3 seconds" has no percentile, no lab/field distinction and no device/network profile ("mid-range Android on 4G"); and it covers only "the launch template", not directory pages. *Fix:* state p75 field LCP or a named lab profile, and scope.
- **medium** FR-191 mixes ambiguous field names (FR-191 vs FR-194) — "a name" (gym name or page name?), "opening hours", "country and city", "about" and "banner image" are not existing gym fields (FR-010 lists none), so the new data model is implied. *Fix:* list the new gym fields once and say how existing gyms are prompted.
- **low** FR-175 "its second factor" — "has no access to any gym until a gym is assigned and its second factor is verified" reads as the gym's second factor. *Fix:* "the account's second factor".
- **low** FR-197 + FR-200 interplay — hreflang alternates are promised while gym content exists in one language, so the "alternate" differs only in chrome; OQ-28 blocks this but FR-200 does not say so. *Fix:* note the dependency in FR-200.

## Scope honesty — adequate

Section 8 rows are explicit and moved-items are marked; non-goals (custom domains, analytics, booking on page, category behavior) are named. Open-items density for V1.7: 4 open OQs (OQ-27..OQ-30), 8 inline ASSUMPTION tags (UJ-12, 4 metrics rows, FR-180, FR-197, FR-201, NFR-025), which is acceptable for a plan whose stories are blocked on a named OQ each. Gaps are in what is silently omitted.

### Findings
- **medium** Suspended gym vs support access is unspecified (FR-132, FR-179, FR-186) — FR-132 says a suspension denies "every staff and member account"; support memberships are staff-scope rows in the same table. It is unstated whether support is denied (and so cannot help a gym fix its SaaS billing situation) or exempted. *Fix:* one sentence in FR-177 or FR-186.
- **medium** No content policy for public pages (FR-195, FR-190) — "There is no pre-moderation"; images and about text go public and are indexed. The takedown path exists but no rule says what is takedown-worthy, who reviews reports, or how a visitor reports a page; "impersonation policy" is cited in OQ-24 but FR-195 only says Super Admin "is notified when a claimed name is a near-match" (near-match undefined). *Fix:* a short policy clause or an explicit NON-GOAL.
- **low** Super Admin second factor is not mentioned (FR-184, FR-185) — support accounts get MFA, and Super Admin can create, assign and reset them, but nothing in V1.7 requires a second factor for Super Admin itself. Possibly addressed elsewhere; if so, cross-reference; if not, say it is out of scope.
- **low** Section 8 table now carries "Moved into V1.7" rows beside genuinely out-of-scope rows; harmless but a reader scanning for exclusions must filter. *Fix:* group the moved rows or add a status column.

## Downstream usability — adequate

IDs run FR-175..FR-203 and NFR-023..NFR-025 contiguously; cross-references resolve (spot-checked FR-072, FR-087, FR-090, FR-091, FR-126, FR-132, NFR-001, NFR-013, NFR-019, NFR-021). The glossary adds support person, GymOS Support row, AAL2, TOTP, landing page, published projection. Gaps: undefined or overloaded terms and missing journeys.

### Findings
- **high** Landing pages and directory have no user journey and no protagonist (§5, FR-189..FR-203) — the only V1.7 journey is UJ-12, which is also tagged `[ASSUMPTION — narrative to confirm]`. The owner who publishes, the visitor who finds a gym, and the Super Admin who takes a page down are never walked through; this is where the sequencing issues above (claim, rename, takedown) would surface. *Fix:* two short UJs (Grace publishes and renames; a prospective member finds a gym in the directory) and a Super Admin assignment/reset journey.
- **medium** "membership" is overloaded and "role limits" is undefined (FR-177, FR-178, FR-183, FR-190, addendum H) — "staff membership" (a `members` row per addendum H), "membership plans", subscription "membership" and Member role all collide. FR-183 excludes support from "staff counts, role limits, notifications, and platform metrics" but not from the member count that sets tier and cap (FR-073, FR-148) or from attendance/occupancy, although addendum H materializes support as a row in the same `members` table. *Fix:* rename ("support assignment" or "gym staff binding"), define "role limits", and add member cap/tier and attendance/occupancy to the exclusion list or say why not needed.
- **medium** Support account provisioning and recovery are not specified (FR-175, FR-185, §12 G) — FR-175 says "its own login (phone or email, as for any staff account)" but staff accounts are provisioned by SMS temporary password (FR-088) which §12 G calls a weak path; first-credential delivery, password reset and TOTP-device-loss for support are not in any FR. Also FR-001/FR-091 ("one phone = one platform user") vs FR-175 ("belongs to GymOS, not to any gym"): a person who is already an Owner/staff elsewhere cannot hold a support account on the same phone, which is unstated. *Fix:* state the provisioning and recovery rules and the identity-collision rule.
- **low** Persona name reuse (UJ-12 vs UJ-5, UJ-10) — "Chidi" is a Super Admin at GymOS HQ in UJ-5/UJ-10 and a support person in UJ-12; a story writer cannot tell whether it is the same person with two roles (contrary to FR-175 / FR-091). *Fix:* a different name or say so.
- **low** UJ-12 contradicts G-16's phrase (G-16, UJ-12) — G-16 promises support "without anyone sharing a gym's credentials", while UJ-12 has Grace reading out her Tara Money credentials to Chidi. The intent (login credentials) is probably different from merchant credentials; say so. Also "she never sees a second support person who also works on her gym on Tuesdays" is not a testable consequence.
- **low** §4.1 role-hierarchy diagram not updated — support appears only in prose of 4.5 ("below Owner"); relative rank to Supervisor ("Supervisor scope ... ranked below Owner") is ambiguous. *Fix:* annotate the diagram.

## Shape fit — adequate

Brownfield, multi-stakeholder B2B plus a public consumer surface: the shape (UJs, FRs by area, release gates per step) fits. The V1.7 update is over-served by dense inline cross-references in places (FR-183 and FR-184 embed design: "A single flag on the membership governs all of it, so no surface needs its own filter", "enforced in the database", "Supabase MFA") that duplicate addendum H; this is consistent with the V1.6 convention (NFR-021) and is low impact. Under-served: the public-facing half has no journeys (see above).

### Findings
- **low** Implementation detail duplicated between FRs and addendum H (FR-183, FR-184, Glossary "anon-safe view") — acceptable where it states a security invariant (database enforcement); not where it prescribes the mechanism ("a single flag"). *Fix:* keep the invariant in the FR, leave the flag to addendum H.
