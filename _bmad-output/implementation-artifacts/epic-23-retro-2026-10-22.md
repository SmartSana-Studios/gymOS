## Process lessons (each from the evidence)

1. **Forward-pointing deferrals acted as the safety control and had no owner at release.** G1-G8 are worded "before the production deploy" or "before support is enabled", and the closing story was never named (`deferred-work.md:1212,1239,1256-1257,1359-1360,1455-1459,1500-1501`). The project rule says these are acceptance criteria; a release-gate list for Epic 23 is the missing artifact.
2. **A closure pass was done for 23-6b only.** Deferrals closed in code by 23-6c, 23-10 and 23-11 are still listed open, and the "resumed" notice was dropped without a decision (H4, R6). The open list both overstates and understates.
3. **Spec status and checkboxes drifted** (H1, H2): status could not be used as audit evidence.
4. **`epic-23-context.md` stopped being the source of truth**: at least four owner decisions (no phone, email-only reset, drift detection, NULL role helpers) live only in specs and `decisions.md` (R1, R2, R5). A restated function body in a 1,400-line migration should carry marked-change comments (D9).
5. **Splitting 23-6 into 23-6b and 23-6c moved risk without moving criteria**: 23-10's velocity lock was inert until 23-6b, tracked only in prose, and 0147 arrived as an unspecified late migration (R7).
6. **Unverified-medium deferrals accumulated on production-facing pieces** (G4, G5, G8, picker window). Each is cheap to prove in a rolled-back local transaction, a method the project already used for 0136 and 0140-0146 (`decisions.md:50,65,81,123`).
7. **Duplication was consistently deferred**, so the same predicate or list is now in 7-8 places (D2, D3, D6). The risk is drift, and the live-gate pair (D2) is the one that matters.
