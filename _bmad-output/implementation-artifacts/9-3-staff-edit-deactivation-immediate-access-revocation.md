# Story 9.3: Staff Edit, Deactivation & Immediate Access Revocation

Status: done

<!-- Note: Validation is optional. Run validate-create-story for quality check before dev-story. -->

## Story

As a gym Owner or Supervisor,
I want to edit a staff member's name/role or deactivate their account, with changes taking effect immediately,
so that role changes and offboarding are secure, not dependent on the staff member's client cooperating.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. **Given** I am an Owner or Supervisor editing a staff member's role, **when** I attempt to raise their role to equal or above my own rung, **then** the edit is rejected by `update_staff_role()`'s ceiling check (same allowlist shape as creation, FR-089) — I cannot promote a Manager to Supervisor, for example. [Source: epics.md Story 9.3]
2. **Given** I am an Owner or Supervisor, **when** I attempt to edit my own role, **then** the edit is rejected outright — self-escalation is structurally impossible, not just discouraged, regardless of what role I'd be changing to. [Source: epics.md Story 9.3]
3. **Given** a staff member I deactivate, **when** I confirm deactivation, **then** a mandatory reason is required, the deactivation is audit-logged with actor + reason + timestamp, and — if they're a Coach — their session notes and any authored workout plans are retained and remain visible to Owner and Manager. [Source: epics.md Story 9.3] **(See Dev Notes "AC #3's Coach-Retention Half Is Already True, and Half Doesn't Exist Yet" — session-note retention needs zero new code; workout-plan retention is currently a no-op since Epic 13 hasn't shipped.)**
4. **Given** a role change or deactivation just saved, **when** the affected staff member's existing session makes its next request, **then** `private.current_member_role()` and `private.current_gym_status()` (the new live-lookup helpers, AD-3) are checked at the RLS/auth-hook layer and reject the stale JWT immediately — no "next token refresh" window; this closes the gap the JWT-claims-only model (FR-003) would otherwise leave open. [Source: epics.md Story 9.3] **(See Dev Notes "AC #4's Real Scope: This Story Cannot Close FR-090's Gap Everywhere" — this AC is only fully satisfiable for the specific RLS surfaces this story retrofits, not the ~30 other role-gated policies across the codebase. Flagged as a [Review][Decision] point, not silently narrowed.)**
5. **Given** the pre-existing `log_audit_event()` function, which currently reads `auth.jwt() ->> 'app_role'` directly, **when** this story ships, **then** `log_audit_event()` is updated to call `private.current_member_role()` instead, so a demoted actor's audit entries reflect their current role, not a stale claim. [Source: epics.md Story 9.3] **(See Dev Notes "AC #5 Is Imprecisely Worded — Here's What Actually Needs to Change" — `current_member_role()` cannot express the check `log_audit_event()` actually performs; the real fix needs a different, new live helper.)**

## Dev Notes

### AC #4's Real Scope: This Story Cannot Close FR-090's Gap Everywhere

FR-090's text promises the gap closes generally: *"immediate revocation requires a server-side check independent of client cooperation... verified at the same auth-hook/RLS layer that already enforces deny-all-by-default."* AD-3's text is similarly general: *"Both [helpers] are called on every RLS evaluation... Role/deactivation/gym-suspension changes take effect on the very next query, no refresh required."* Taken fully literally, this means **every** RLS policy and every `SECURITY DEFINER` function gating on role or gym status across the whole codebase.

That is not what this story actually does, and it would be a mistake to claim otherwise. A full grep (`grep -rn "auth.jwt() ->> 'app_role'" supabase/migrations/`) turns up **33 call sites across ~20 migration files** — payments, classes/bookings, subscriptions, coach-portal, quiet-gym-alerts, refunds, and more — none of which this story touches. AD-3's own text already anticipates and accepts this reality: *"A CI grep-lint gate forbids **new** `auth.jwt() ->> 'app_role'` call sites... 27 existing migrations reference it today and are grandfathered."* Incremental adoption, not a single-story rewrite, is the explicitly designed migration path.

**What this story actually closes**: the one RLS policy most directly load-bearing for "can a deactivated/demoted staff member still act as staff at all" — `gym_staff_read_own_members` (retrofitted in Task 5), which gates the Staff List's own read (`listStaff()`) and is a reasonable proxy for "the staff-management surface itself." A demoted-to-Coach or deactivated Manager's *next* `/settings/staff` page load (or any other query hitting this specific policy) is correctly denied/filtered, with no token refresh — this is directly, narrowly testable (Task 7's dedicated regression) and is what AC #4's own literal BDD scenario describes.

**What stays open, honestly**: the other ~30 policies still trust the JWT claim. A demoted Manager whose *role* changed (say, to Receptionist) but who is not yet deactivated will, for up to an hour (`config.toml`'s `jwt_expiry = 3600`), still pass **every other** role-gated RLS check as if they were still a Manager — e.g. they could still `verify` a manual payment or edit a membership plan, both gated by policies this story doesn't touch. This is a real, material gap in FR-090's own promise, not a cosmetic one. **This is the single most important thing for the reviewer/user to weigh in on before or during this story's dev-story pass**: either (a) accept this narrowed scope as this story's actual, honestly-scoped deliverable (recommended — matches AD-3's own incremental-adoption design and this story's own reasonable size), with the remaining ~30 call sites tracked as a `deferred-work.md` entry for a future, larger "retrofit the rest of the RLS surface to AD-3" story; or (b) expand this story's scope materially to retrofit a larger set of policies (which ones? all staff-writable surfaces? all of them?) — a much bigger, differently-shaped story than what's drafted here. **Flagged as a [Review][Decision] point, not silently resolved** — recommendation is (a).

### AC #5 Is Imprecisely Worded — Here's What Actually Needs to Change

AC #5's text says `log_audit_event()` "currently reads `auth.jwt() ->> 'app_role'` directly" and should be "updated to call `private.current_member_role()` instead, so a demoted actor's audit entries reflect their current role, not a stale claim." Reading the actual function (`0061:192-259`, already re-created once by Story 9.1 for the actor-name fallback fix), the **only** `auth.jwt() ->> 'app_role'` call site left is line 235: `v_caller_is_super_admin := coalesce((auth.jwt() ->> 'app_role') = 'super_admin', false);` — used solely to decide whether the caller is exempt from the `p_gym_id` cross-tenant match check (a Super Admin can legitimately write an audit event for a gym that isn't their own, since they have none). **This check has nothing to do with "a demoted actor's audit entries reflect their current role"** — `log_audit_event()` never stores the actor's role in the audit row at all (only `actor_id`/`actor_display_name`); the AC's own stated rationale doesn't match what the code actually does at that call site.

More importantly, **`private.current_member_role()` cannot literally replace this check** — it returns `member_role` (`member`/`coach`/`receptionist`/`manager`/`owner`/`supervisor`, per `0001_extensions_and_enums.sql:10` plus `0060`'s added value), which has no `super_admin` value. Super Admin status lives on a separate column, `users.is_super_admin` (per `0061`'s own comment: "Super Admin is a separate `users.is_super_admin` flag, never a `member_role` value"). A Super Admin has no `members` row at all in the typical case, so `current_member_role()` would simply return `NULL` for them — silently and incorrectly failing the `is_super_admin` check for every real Super Admin, a functional regression, not a fix.

**What this story actually does instead** (Task 6): add `private.is_super_admin_live()`, a new live-lookup sibling to the existing JWT-claim-only `private.is_super_admin()` (`0010:18-24`, left untouched — it's used elsewhere and this story doesn't audit every one of its call sites), reading `users.is_super_admin` directly via `auth.uid()`. This is the same *spirit* as AC #5 (replace a stale-JWT-claim read with a live lookup at this exact call site) while being the technically correct helper for what the code actually checks. Recorded here and in `docs/decisions.md` per this story's own "flag deviations, don't silently follow imprecise text" discipline (matching Story 9.2's own "Why Evolution API, Not Twilio" precedent for the same kind of situation).

### Deactivation Needs a Ceiling Too, By Direct Precedent

This story's own ACs (from epics.md) describe a role-ceiling for **edits** (AC #1) but say nothing explicit about a ceiling for **deactivation** — FR-089's text is similarly silent on this specific point, saying only "an Owner or Supervisor can... deactivate (soft-delete) a staff account," without an explicit target-role restriction. Left unceilinged, a Supervisor could deactivate an Owner (locking out the gym's actual owner) or another Supervisor — a real privilege-abuse path, and the *exact* gap Story 9.2's own code review found and fixed for `staff_account_for_reset()` (password reset) on 2026-08-19: *"an unceilinged Supervisor could otherwise force-reset an Owner's password."* Deactivation is at least as security-sensitive as a password reset (arguably more — it's irreversible in this story's own scope, per the no-reactivation-path note below), so this story adopts the same ceiling `staff_account_for_reset()` already established: **Owner may deactivate any non-owner staff role; Supervisor may deactivate Manager/Receptionist/Coach only, never Owner or another Supervisor.** Flagged as a direct, reasoned extrapolation from an already-accepted precedent, not a silent assumption.

### Self-Deactivation: EXPERIENCE.md's Silent Gap

AD-16's UI spec explicitly disables Role editing on your own row ("Editing your own row: the Role field is present but disabled") but says nothing about whether the Deactivate button/action is available on your own row at all. Left unaddressed, an Owner or Supervisor could deactivate their own staff account via this exact UI — locking themselves out of the dashboard entirely (per AC #4/Task 5, immediately, on their own next request), with no reactivation path in this story's scope and no guarantee another Owner/Supervisor exists to fix it. This story blocks self-deactivation outright at the RPC layer (Task 4), matching AC #2's own "structurally impossible, not just discouraged" framing for the analogous self-role-edit case. Flagged, not silently decided — if the user prefers allowing self-deactivation (e.g. an Owner stepping down deliberately, with a separate ownership-transfer flow), that changes this story's Task 4/12/14 materially, and a reactivation path becomes a hard dependency rather than an optional follow-up.

### Self-Edit Scope: Name-Only Self-Edits Are Not Blocked
